## 5. The init-state heterogeneity finding

Extending the pool from 100 to 160 states (D.1 Option A) introduced two
sub-populations. Generated states are **significantly easier**:

```
success rate:  shipped 0.660 (n=200)   generated 0.800 (n=100)   z=+2.51  p=0.012
```

**Among successes**, however, the score distributions largely agree:

| statistic | shipped | generated | pooled KS p |
|---|---|---|---|
| per-rollout mean `err_norm` | 0.7724 | 0.7718 | 0.473 — same |
| S_k reach | 0.977 | 0.953 | **0.0059 — different** |
| S_k grasp | 1.069 | 1.064 | 0.313 — same |
| S_k transport | 1.029 | 0.996 | **0.0219 — different** |
| S_k release | 1.012 | 1.014 | 0.897 — same |

**Consequence for calibration.** The cal/eval split is `global_index % 2`, which
interleaves, so calibration and evaluation contain *identical* mixtures
(50 shipped + 25 generated each). Exchangeability between cal and test therefore
holds — a mixture is a valid distribution provided both sides draw from it.

The mixture also yields **lower** thresholds than shipped-only (task 8 reach:
1.192 vs 1.539), i.e. more alarms. The bias is **conservative**; it cannot
inflate coverage.

**Required disclosure in the calibration artefact and the paper:**

> q_k for tasks 0, 1 and 8 are calibrated on a mixture of LIBERO's shipped init
> states (idx 0–99) and 50 environment-reset-generated states (idx 100–149).
> Coverage claims hold with respect to that mixture, not LIBERO's native init
> distribution alone.

## 6. Is 3 tasks enough for CP calibration?

**For calibration: yes, comfortably. The binding constraint is evaluation.**

Calibration consumes *successes*; evaluation consumes *failures*.

| requirement | threshold | task 0 | task 1 | task 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| finite q_k | n_cal ≥ 39 | 82 ✅ | 87 ✅ | 102 ✅ |
| q_k strictly below sample max | n_cal ≥ 79 | 82 ✅ | 87 ✅ | 102 ✅ |
| failures for detection rate | as many as possible | 47 | 41 | **22** ⚠️ |

At α=0.10, K=4 (Bonferroni → α_k=0.025), cal_frac 0.8. **All three tasks can be
calibrated today.** Task 8's 22 failures give a detection-rate CI of ~±0.21.

A 4th task at p≈0.50 would add ~100 failures and roughly halve that interval.
It is a *precision* improvement, not a prerequisite.
